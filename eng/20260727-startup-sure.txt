A startup idea isn't validated until someone actually uses it.

I talked about it with a person I'm collaborating with and we came to different conclusions. 
That gave me this question: 
how much does the MVP matter in understanding whether an idea works?

There are several ways to validate a software idea, and they don't exclude each other:

- study the market, to understand whether there's real demand and who the competitors are;
- run surveys or interviews with potential users;
- build a landing page to collect contacts and measure interest;
- develop an MVP and collect real usage data.

From experience, only the last one gives a definitive answer.

The others tell you the idea is liked. The MVP tells you whether the product gets used.

Until you put a first version on the market and see users coming back day after day, you only have hints. 
Even if all the other signals are positive.

I admit I'm influenced by my "always think of the worst", which I usually apply when I develop.

What's your take?
