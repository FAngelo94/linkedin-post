Binance suffered the biggest theft in crypto history: 6.3 billion dollars.

It happened on October 29th, in a few hours.
On November 3rd Binance published the report on how they pulled it off.

The most fascinating thing? The how.
No hidden flaw, no movie-style exploit.

In September a Binance engineer is contacted on LinkedIn.
First interview on video with a fake HR person.
Second interview in chat with the fake CEO of a company that doesn't exist.

Then an apparently harmless request that several of you will have come across: download an assignment to complete.

It was a repository with malware inside, run on the company laptop.
From there, a month later, they reached the wallets and emptied them.

How did he fall for it?
Actually the fake company, to gain the victim's trust, built an entire credible story: a realistic fake website, fake social profiles, fake interviews and fake company documents.

A few months ago something similar happened to me too but nowhere near this sophisticated and so I didn't fall for it.

It's a story many companies can learn from, often the weak link in a company's security remains the human being, which is why it's worth paying attention to:
- investing in training, but done well, not the usual boring theoretical lessons on phishing emails
- organizing user permissions so that they have access only to what is strictly necessary for their job

Personally, after this story, and my similar experience and others I've read on LinkedIn, in my next interviews I'm definitely not downloading anything anymore xD
