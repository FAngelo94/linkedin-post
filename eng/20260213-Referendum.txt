A thought on the Justice Referendum

In recent weeks I studied the topic: articles, videos, pro and con interventions.
Because when I vote I want to do it with my own head, not with the politician who presents their idea better.

Digging into the topic I realized one thing: we are delegating to millions of people an extremely technical decision, which affects the institutional architecture of the country.

I had the time (and willingness) to study.
But realistically, how many will?

The risk is that the winning argument won’t be the best one, but the simplest narrative.
Or the loudest one.

The referendum is a very powerful democratic tool.
But it works well only when those who vote have adequate tools to understand what they are changing. Otherwise it becomes a huge political lever, usable to change delicate parts of the system with slogans.

I see it this way:
in my work, if a company asks me to develop a webapp, I don’t make managers vote on which architecture to use or which database to choose. I listen to them about business goals, but technical choices belong to those who have the skills to evaluate them.

Here we are doing the opposite.

What do you think?
