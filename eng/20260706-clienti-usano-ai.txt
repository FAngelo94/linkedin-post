"How awful, websites made with AI by non-experts"

That's the gist of more and more posts I see on LinkedIn where some professionals seem to even be annoyed at being contacted to fix sites made with AI.

For me it depends on the type of client. If it's a big company showing me an enormous AI-made site full of holes to patch, then yes, I wouldn't be too thrilled.

If instead it's an individual, or a small business, then I don't mind working with, and maybe doing a bit of training for, someone who made a site with AI without having the skills to do it well.

Because I kind of see myself in them, in the sense that I too like to try my hand at jobs, often manual ones, that let's say aren't really my core business, often making a bit of a mess.

And in those cases I'd like to have a professional who not only fixes what I did but also gives me some advice to do better next time.

In software development I want to be that kind of professional.

So if you've put your hands on a website or an app with AI, and the result is a bit patched together, for me it's not a problem.

Let's see what can be fixed, and how to do it better from there on.
