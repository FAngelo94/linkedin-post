What if instead of a hard disk you had a QR code to save your photos?

Anyone who hasn't read the news might think I wrote it wrong (or that I smoked something before writing), but it's all true: a team of researchers created a QR code so small it can be engraved on a ceramic film, with pixels of only 49 nanometers. This means that over 2 terabytes of data could be stored on an A4 sheet.

Moreover, being made of ceramic materials, this QR code is extremely resistant to heat, pressure and time.
Obviously it also doesn't require power or refreshing to retain the data, which means it could last for centuries, perhaps even millennia.

When I read the news two things came to mind:
1. The engraved tablets seen in many museums (which are also mentioned at the end of the article)
2. You could store 2 TB of photos on an A4 sheet, making it easy (because it would take up little space) and cheap to build a family album that lasts generations

Ok, actually it's not like we can all create or read this kind of QR code at home — for example you need an electron microscope to read it — but maybe in the long run you could pay a service to do it for us.
