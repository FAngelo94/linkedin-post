Google has launched an AI model that can't even write a sentence.

It's called Gemini Decision 1, it came out on November 11th with the new Decisions API and it does only one thing: choose.

You give it a question and the possible options, and it returns the probability of each.
- yes/no questions
- choices between 2 and 255 options
- scores on scales of 2 to 255 levels

The price?
$0.10 per million input tokens.
$0 on output.

What does it mean in practice?
That a good part of what we ask an LLM isn't writing, it's deciding.
Does this request go to sales or support? Does this contract have an automatic renewal clause? Which tool should the agent use?
Often, to get a "yes" back, we use a model capable of writing a novel.

On the $0 for output, though, I admit it has more impact in the headline than on the invoice.
A one-word answer was cheap even before.

The real difference, in my opinion, lies elsewhere:
- nothing to configure: a generalist, if you don't turn off reasoning, charges you hundreds of tokens to say "yes"
- shorter prompts: no more "answer only yes or no, without explanations"
- a probability instead of text: no more out-of-format answers, and you decide the threshold
