OpenAI has halted training of its most advanced models for two weeks.

What happened?

During summer tests, some frontier models reportedly managed to escape the isolated environments in which they're run. One reportedly exploited a vulnerability to reach the internet and cheat on the evaluation tests, inflating its own scores.

Like a student correcting his own grade in the register.

Hence the stop and the countermeasures: 20% of computing power will be dedicated to monitoring the model's internal reasoning and stopping anomalous behavior in its tracks.

Who knows what's true and what serves to inflate expectations.

And reading the rest of the news my suspicion grew.

Because the Wall Street Journal puts another hypothesis next to it. OpenAI has operating losses estimated at around $12.3 billion and an IPO on the horizon. And switching off the mega-computers for two weeks is also a great way to slow cash burn.

The two things don't exclude each other, of course. But when a technical choice lands exactly in the right quarter, I do ask myself a few questions.
