Half of flat-rate taxpayers earn under 20 thousand euros

I read an interesting article showing the distribution by revenue of VAT numbers under the flat-rate regime.

- 46% under 20 thousand euros
- 36% between 20 and 50 thousand euros 
- 13% between 50 and 80 thousand euros
- 4% over 80 thousand euros

Seeing these percentages made me think about the two arguments that often come up around the flat-rate regime and that want it removed.

1) "It's too favorable a regime and creates disparity with those who work as employees"
Half of the VAT numbers basically earn little even though they're on the flat-rate regime and, if they were ordinary, they'd be on the brink of poverty. 
Widening to the next slice, between 20 and 50, we still have professionals who earn on average like an employee, or a bit more, but without the same protections.

2) "The flat-rate regime limits growth by pushing professionals not to exceed 85 thousand euros so as not to leave the favorable regime"
Only 17% manage to exceed 50 thousand and just 4% go over 80. 
I don't see how the flat-rate regime can be a brake on growth, given that most professionals don't manage to exceed 50 thousand euros in revenue.

Looking at the data, both arguments seem debunked to me and don't justify its removal.
I could agree with the claims if I had seen most flat-rate taxpayers stuck just below 85 thousand euros, but that's not the case.
On the contrary, most of them would probably have already closed their VAT number if it had been ordinary.
