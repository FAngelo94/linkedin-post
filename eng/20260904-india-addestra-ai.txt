Who teaches artificial intelligence to fold a towel?
A 21-year-old girl, in India, with a camera mounted on her head.

Her name is Rani, she has an engineering degree and lives in Karur.

She spends her days filming herself folding towels: about 90 videos a day.

What are they for?
They're "egocentric data". First-person footage, annotated gesture by gesture, used to explain to a robot how a human hand really moves.

The company she works for has 2,600 employees, 300 hired in the last month alone. The clients are almost all American.

The salary?
Between 180 and 220 euros a month. Freelancers get around 2 euros an hour.

When we talk about models that "learn on their own" we imagine something automatic. 
Instead, in some cases, behind it there's someone repeating the same gesture for eight hours, because that gesture counts as data.

And there's a detail that stays in my head: the Indian government itself wonders how long it will last, because these are the first jobs that will be automated.

They're teaching a machine to do exactly the job they're being paid for.
