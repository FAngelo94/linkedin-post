What is a Cookie?

Anyone reading this post will have accepted who knows how many, but how many people know what they are in practical terms?
A small text file that a site saves in your browser, that's it.

And what are they for?
It depends

The "technical" ones are for useful things: keeping you logged in, remembering the language, the cart of an e-commerce. Without them you'd have to log in again on every page.

The "less good" cookies are third-party ones, placed not by the site you're visiting, but by external companies, almost always advertising.
You search for a flight to London on a travel site. That site has a cookie from an ad network. You go on Instagram, same network, same cookie.
Result: ads for flights to London everywhere.

It's not magic, it's not your phone listening to you, it's a "little cookie" that follows you from one site to another building a profile of your interests.
(Although sometimes I'd swear I've received ads for things I've never searched for, only talked about)

Then there's the theatre of cookie banners.
GDPR says you must give explicit consent, in theory.
In practice, "Accept all" is a big colorful button, "Customize" is a grey link that opens 15 submenus, which I often don't even notice.
It's called a dark pattern, and it's designed specifically to make you click yes.
