Dear HR and colleagues, a question 👇

Does a take-home technical test still make sense?

I still see positions where the "homework" is sent to be completed within X days.
After 8 years of experience, online projects and public code to discuss, investing hours (or days) on a test seems poorly aligned with the current context, as well as disrespectful of a professional's time.

Moreover, today with a subscription to Claude or ChatGPT that test in many cases can be easily done by AI.
And so you risk selecting whoever has the better tool, not who is the better developer.

If you really want to understand how a developer works, the most useful thing is to talk together.
See how they reason, how they approach a problem, how they make decisions.
Maybe by looking at a project implemented in the past.

In my case I also propose a concrete alternative designed for those who want to use my subscription formula:
let's start collaborating for a week.

If you're convinced by how I work and how I make decisions, you pay and we continue.
If you don't feel comfortable, we part ways.
(for more info: https://angelofalci.com/)

What do you think?
