Tech layoffs in 2026 have already exceeded all of 2025.

Five months early.

In 2025, 122,606 jobs were cut across 278 tech companies, over the whole year.
As of August 6, 2026 we're already at 125,759 (126,305 according to Layoffs.fyi).

Among those who cut are names like Salesforce, LinkedIn, Etsy, Zillow, Rapid7.

What does it mean in practice?
That 2026 isn't a "normal" year with a few more cuts than usual.
It's a year in which tech companies are redesigning their headcount, or at least trying to, faster than they manage to explain it.

And the official reasons?
Almost always the same: efficiency, reorganization, "focus on AI".

But then AI alone doesn't write or run a product.
So it's hard to tell where they're simply cutting and where they're actually investing.

For those who work in the industry, did you have the sense that this year would be so tough?
And if you work in HR or recruiting, how are you experiencing it from the other side of the table?
