In September Stack Overflow received fewer than a thousand questions.

In the whole month.
In March 2014, its record month, there were 207,204.

The decline began well before ChatGPT, of course, but AI delivered the final blow.
Today we obviously ask our questions to AIs.

And this made me think about something I want to share.

Models learned to solve bugs also thanks to millions of threads in which someone had banged their head against it before us.
The absurd bug that only shows up with that version, on that operating system, behind that proxy.
One person found it, another answered, a third corrected in the comments.
And it became common heritage, accessible to anyone with the same problem.

Today we solve that bug in a chat.
If it goes well, the solution stays there, private.
If it goes badly, neither of the two finds it.

As if in medicine there were no more exchange of experience among doctors on rare cases and everyone had to deal with it alone from a certain year onward.

Tomorrow's frameworks will have documentation and source code, but their absurd bugs maybe won't have been told by anyone.

With models that reason, and develop code, better and better, maybe the rare bug problem will no longer exist.

What do you think?
And when was the last time you looked something up on Stack Overflow?
