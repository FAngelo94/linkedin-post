AI allows anyone to write code.
The problem is managing it without creating a mess.

That’s why one of the first things I recommend to people who develop only using AI is to use GitHub.
Not just to "save code", but to have versions, history, backups, and at least some control when something breaks.


For those who’ve been developing for years, this sounds obvious.
For beginners, though, it often isn’t.
And that’s exactly why the second video in my beginner-friendly guide will be about GitHub.

I discovered GitHub only towards the end of my final year of my Bachelor's degree in Computer Engineering, almost by chance.
Since then, it has become the home of my projects, with over 100 repositories by now.

Before that, for my first projects, I used to save multiple local copies of the code to have a sort of "manual versioning"—it makes me laugh/cry just thinking about it now.

I’ve always found it incredible that after 5 years of IT technical school and 3 years of university, no one had ever seriously talked to me about GitHub or version control systems.

Which is crazy, considering how fundamental they are in real-world work.
