How much stuff does Google ask for to publish an app on the Play Store?

In these days I've gotten ahead with publishing Autochess of Ages, the game I told you about a few days ago.

When I published my last app, about ten years ago, all you needed was the APK, a few screenshots and a content questionnaire.

Today?
- Identity verification with a document and phone number
- Mandatory privacy policy
- A page explaining how to delete the account and the user's data
- Data safety form: what data you collect, why, whether you share it, whether it's encrypted
- Questionnaires on target audience, ads, test credentials for reviewers
- Target API 36 (Android 16), mandatory from August 31, 2026
- I probably forgot something

The part that surprised me most?
For personal accounts created after November 2023 you first need a closed test with at least 12 testers, enrolled for 14 consecutive days.
And from 2026 Google even checks that the app is really being used.
Luckily I managed to recover my old developer account so I shouldn't have to go through this step.

I understand the goal: fewer junk apps and fewer scams on the store.
Then though I often come across misleading ads for games that promise experiences that don't match reality.
Or, in other cases, games that blatantly violate the copyright of other works and I wonder how on earth they got past the store's review.

A bit exaggerated for nothing if there are still apps that manage to pass review while breaking the rules?
How do those who often develop mobile experience it?
